Quantinuum maps Nexus developer platform path towards fault tolerant computing

By PhotonDispatch Staff · Published 6 November 2026 · Last updated 11 November 2026

Quantinuum has unveiled a roadmap for its Nexus developer platform, setting out how the software is intended to evolve alongside the company’s quantum hardware towards a fault tolerant computing era.

The company announced the plan on 5 November. It describes Nexus as a platform that brings together programming, validation, compilation and execution, with a roadmap intended to automate more of the work involved in building and deploying quantum applications.

The plan is aligned with successive generations of Quantinuum hardware, including Apollo, which the company says is planned as its first universal, fully fault tolerant quantum computer in 2029. That timing is a company target, not a guarantee of delivery.

Quantinuum divides the platform’s intended development into three broad stages: develop, optimise and deploy. The develop stage is expected to include pre built primitives, fault tolerant libraries and domain specific tools, with increasing automation of error correction encoding, compilation and resource management.

The optimise stage is intended to provide tools for analysing runtime behaviour, resource requirements and application performance. Future releases are expected to add further error suppression, real time monitoring and more automated error recovery.

The deploy stage is designed to coordinate quantum and classical computing across Quantinuum processors, high performance computing systems, cloud infrastructure and third party tools. The aim is to let organisations integrate quantum workflows into existing computing environments.

The company says Nexus already supports more than 200 organisations, up 45 per cent in 2026. It also reports that job submissions increased tenfold in the 12 months through August 2026 compared with the preceding 12 month period.

Quantinuum says developers can already apply supported error correction techniques to programs and run supported programs on its Helios system. A further release planned for December is expected to expand hybrid workflows, add supported error correction codes and provide execution analytics.

The roadmap is a statement of intended product development. Its future capabilities, adoption and hardware milestones remain subject to delivery and should be read as company plans.

Quantinuum later said it and RIKEN had upgraded Japan's Reimei computer to a 56-qubit System Model H2.
