IBM, NVIDIA and Fermilab plan a co-design platform that puts GPUs next to IBM quantum computers

By PhotonDispatch Staff · Published 17 November 2026 · Last updated 17 November 2026

Short answer: IBM, NVIDIA and Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory plan to build a shared platform for designing modular quantum computers alongside classical supercomputers. IBM Research posted the news on X from the SC26 supercomputing conference in Chicago on Monday, and the details are in IBM's press release.

According to the release, the platform will co-locate NVIDIA DGX B200 systems with IBM quantum computers, including those powered by IBM Quantum Nighthawk. IBM describes Nighthawk as its most powerful processor so far, running circuits of up to 7,500 two-qubit operations on 120 qubits at up to 100,000 samples per second.

The aim is to test what is needed for several quantum processors to work together and for classical computing to scale alongside them. Fermilab's Superconducting Quantum Materials and Systems (SQMS) Center will contribute its work on ultracoherent quantum hardware. Fermilab and IBM also plan to study calculations in high-energy and nuclear physics that classical machines cannot handle alone, such as lattice gauge theories, neutrino interactions and nuclear simulations.

Jerry Chow, IBM Fellow and chief technology officer of quantum-centric computing, said the partners want to explore how real problems "can help us shape the modular, interconnected architectures that will define the next generation of quantum computers".

The release describes intentions rather than a working system. It does not say when the platform will be running, where it will be housed or how much it will cost, so the useful milestones to watch are the first published experiments from the shared setup.

Note: This is an announced plan. The companies gave no start date, cost or site details, and no results have been published yet.
