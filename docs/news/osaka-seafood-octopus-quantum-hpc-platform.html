University of Osaka links its OCTOPUS supercomputer to in-house quantum computers in SEAFOOD platform

By PhotonDispatch Staff · Published 12 November 2026 · Last updated 12 November 2026

Short answer: The University of Osaka has built SEAFOOD, a platform that lets its OCTOPUS supercomputer and its own superconducting quantum computers run parts of the same calculation. The university says SEAFOOD will begin operating in December 2026, with trial access for selected research teams in fiscal 2027.

According to the university press release published by The Quantum Insider on 11 November, SEAFOOD connects OCTOPUS, run by the university's D3 Center, with quantum computers developed and operated by its Center for Quantum Information and Quantum Biology (QIQB) on a separate campus. The link uses OQTOPUS, open-source software developed by QIQB and partners, so programs on OCTOPUS can send work straight to the quantum machines and get results back. The university calls it the first case in Japan of joining a domestically developed quantum computer with a supercomputer at the same university.

As a proof of concept, the team used quantum-selected configuration interaction, in which the quantum computer picks out important electron configurations and the supercomputer does the matrix calculations. In a test on four hydrogen atoms, OCTOPUS and QIQB's Urchin quantum computer ran the whole calculation as one workflow and produced a ground-state energy that matched a conventional reference calculation. That is a small benchmark molecule, chosen to show the plumbing works rather than to beat classical methods.

The D3 Center's announcement describes SEAFOOD as jointly developed by the D3 Center and QIQB. The release says lessons from the fiscal 2027 trials will be used to improve job scheduling and to explore wider access through national programmes, and that using open-source software and standard interfaces may help other institutions build similar systems.

Note: Results described here are as reported by the researchers and companies involved and have not been independently replicated by PhotonDispatch.
