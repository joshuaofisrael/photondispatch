IBM announces Quantum Nighthawk r3, built for 10,000 operations on 520 qubits

By PhotonDispatch Staff · Published 11 November 2026 · Last updated 11 November 2026

IBM announced on 11 November that its next Quantum Nighthawk processor, r3, will be built to execute 10,000 quantum operations across 520 programmable qubits. In a post on X, IBM Research wrote: "@IBM announces IBM Quantum Nighthawk r3, a new processor to support 10k quantum operations on 520 programmable qubits. A new managed workflow experience on the IBM Quantum Platform brings classical and quantum compute together in a single environment."

The IBM Newsroom release, dated from Yorktown Heights, New York, says Nighthawk r3 is designed for 10,000 operations on 520 programmable qubits, with 2,034 total physical qubits. It keeps the square-lattice connectivity and fast operations of the current Nighthawk r2 and adds 10 chip-to-chip interconnects, called l-couplers, intended to join processors into larger systems. In 2027, IBM plans to link two Nighthawk r3 processors into a single 1,040-qubit system.

Jay Gambetta, director of IBM Research and an IBM Fellow, said in the release: "Useful quantum computing hinges on whether results can be delivered with accuracy and speed, both are fundamental. Nighthawk is already showing us what that looks like today. As we scale this architecture to larger and more powerful quantum computers, the capabilities of quantum computation will reach new levels as we integrate more closely with classical resources and march ahead to fault tolerance."

Nighthawk r2, introduced in September, can deliver up to 100,000 samples per second and support computations with more than 7,500 quantum operations on 120 qubits, IBM said. Clients and partners including E.ON, Algorithmiq and Q-CTRL have run more than 34 billion circuits on r2 and reported speedups of up to 19 times over previous IBM processors, according to the company. IBM said E.ON reported a 15 times speedup on r2 in quantum machine learning for energy-grid optimisation, and that IBM itself saw a 12 times speedup, versus earlier work on IBM Quantum Heron, in a magnetic-materials experiment with Oak Ridge National Laboratory.

IBM also said it has connected Nighthawk r1 processors using four l-couplers, reaching 99.3% quantum state transfer fidelity toward a 99.9% target, and has connected two cryogenic modules into a single system for the first time, reaching operating temperature in under five days and cooling below 15 millikelvin.

The same announcement describes a managed IBM Quantum Platform environment that unites high-performance classical computing with quantum workflows. IBM said users can now plug quantum work into classical resources on the platform, and cited faster sample-based quantum diagonalization work with RIKEN and automated processing of 608 million samples with Cleveland Clinic and RIKEN. Those results are IBM's account of partner workloads.

Nighthawk r3 is a planned next-generation processor, not a machine already running customer jobs at 520 qubits. The 1,040-qubit linked system is a 2027 plan. The performance figures for r2 are IBM's, including partner-reported speedups.
