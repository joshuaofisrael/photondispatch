Quantinuum and RIKEN say they have upgraded Japan's Reimei computer to a 56-qubit H2 system

By PhotonDispatch Staff · Published 11 November 2026 · Last updated 11 November 2026

Quantinuum said on 10 November that it and RIKEN have upgraded the Reimei quantum computer to Quantinuum's System Model H2. In a company blog post, Quantinuum described Reimei as having 56 fully connected high-fidelity qubits and said the machine, at RIKEN's campus, is being used with the Fugaku supercomputer and the ROQUO GPU supercomputer as a hybrid platform. The post says the upgrade "bring[s] quantum advantage into sight." That is Quantinuum's claim, not an independently verified milestone.

According to the post, Quantinuum Nexus coordinates the three machines so that jobs running on classical infrastructure can submit quantum tasks to Reimei and fold the results back into a larger calculation. Fugaku is described as handling large-scale classical modelling, ROQUO as supporting tensor-network simulation, AI training and massively parallel computation, and Reimei as performing selected calculations involving quantum states that become expensive to represent classically.

The company said two frontier projects are already under way. One, labelled GenQAI for materials discovery, is intended to map a material's phase diagram by combining quantum simulation, classical tensor networks and generative AI. Quantinuum said the team has already collected hardware data for a 6 by 6 two-dimensional spin model, trained a Transformer model for zero-noise extrapolation, and used a generative model to sample conditions that have not yet been simulated. The second project, with researchers from RIKEN, NVIDIA and Quantinuum, is described as a hybrid workflow for biomolecular chemistry, with an initial target of zinc porphyrin and a focus on excited-state properties rather than ground-state searches. Those project descriptions and early results are as stated by Quantinuum.

The post said that once the projects conclude, Reimei will start regular operations under a Test User Program. It also said the Reimei deployment is supported by Japan's New Energy and Industrial Technology Development Organization as part of a programme on integrated use of quantum computers and supercomputers. Quantinuum did not, in this announcement, report a published benchmark that would settle whether the upgraded system has reached quantum advantage.
