Supercomputers can now book a quantum processor like a GPU, and IBM, Quantinuum and Pasqal are already on board

By PhotonDispatch Staff

Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Short answer: The job scheduler that runs most of the world's supercomputers is getting native support for quantum processors. NVIDIA says the next Slurm release lets a centre treat a QPU as just another resource that researchers can reserve, next to the CPUs and GPUs they already use.

"At #SC26, we've expanded Slurm to bring QPUs into the workflows HPC centers already run," NVIDIA's AI infrastructure account posted on X. "This enables scientists to bring quantum computing into their research domains without friction and allows QPU builders to reuse one integration across every deployment."

What changes. According to NVIDIA's announcement, until now every new QPU or supercomputing centre meant a fresh software integration. The new release builds on the existing QRMI and QDMI quantum-HPC interfaces so a QPU maker can write "a single interface to their hardware for every supercomputing center, through a simple file". NVIDIA's technical blog says this works through Slurm's new Hierarchical Resources (HRES) plugins, which schedule QPUs as cluster-level resources, and Pasqal has published an example configuration for its own processors.

Who is in. NVIDIA says the expansion is already being adopted by Alice & Bob, IBM, IQM, Pasqal, Quantinuum and MQSC. It also says nearly 30 supercomputing centres worldwide are in the process of deploying more than 50 quantum processors. Slurm 26.11 "will be available later this month", the company says.

Why it matters. Scheduling sounds dull, but it is one of the practical blockers to hybrid quantum-classical computing: if a chemist cannot request a QPU the same way they request GPUs, the hardware sits idle. A shared plugin route means vendors stop rebuilding the same integration for each site. We covered other SC26 quantum news, including IBM, NVIDIA and Fermilab's co-design platform and IQM's Halocene upgrade programme.

Note: Slurm 26.11 had not been released at the time of writing; NVIDIA says it will be available later this month. Adoption claims are NVIDIA's.
