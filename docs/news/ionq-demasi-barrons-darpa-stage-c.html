IonQ CEO DeMasi tells Barron’s the firm is building a Western quantum ecosystem and cites DARPA’s final stage

By PhotonDispatch Staff

Published 7 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

On 6 November 2026 IonQ said on X that chief executive Niccolo de Masi had told Barron's the company is building a quantum ecosystem for the United States, the Five Eyes and NATO, and that IonQ had reached the final stage of DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative. Those lines are IonQ's. The stage itself was set out a month earlier, by DARPA.

What DARPA said on 7 October

DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative is a government test of whether any quantum computing approach can reach what DARPA calls utility-scale operation, meaning the computation is worth more than it costs, by 2033. On 7 October 2026 DARPA said four more organizations had entered the final stage, Stage C: Atom Computing of Boulder, Colorado (neutral atoms), Diraq of Sydney (silicon spin qubits), IBM of Yorktown Heights (modular superconducting processors) and IonQ of College Park, Maryland (trapped ions). DARPA said they join Microsoft and PsiQuantum, which had entered Stage C through an earlier pilot, US2QC. Micah Stoutimore, who manages the program, put the change in one sentence: "Stage C is where we begin moving from plans to proof." DARPA says that in this stage its team works with each company to test whether the proposed machine can be built as designed and operated as intended. It also says the program is not a competition to pick a winner. We have separate notes on Atom Computing and Diraq. Those stories are not the Stage C announcement.

The published ceilings

DARPA's program page, whose performer list is marked current as of 8 October 2026, says Stage A work can be awarded up to $1 million, Stage B up to $15 million, and Stage C up to $300 million. IonQ's release the same week says the company signed a Stage C agreement with a potential value of up to $300 million, and that money beyond the initial obligation depends on future appropriations and later DARPA funding decisions. The release does not state the size of that initial obligation. IonQ says the Stage C work runs through 2029 and will put more than one generation of its Superion systems through DARPA's tests. IonQ labels those statements as forward-looking. They are the company's plan, not a DARPA finding that a Superion machine has passed the test.

Who else is on the same list

On that 8 October list, IonQ is in Stage C and Quantinuum, the other trapped-ion company readers meet most often here, is in Stage B. Google Quantum AI is in Stage A, on superconducting transmons. IBM is in Stage C with IonQ. The trapped-ion topic collects both IonQ and Quantinuum. DARPA notes that a company appears only while it has an active scope of work, and that the list is not updated in real time. Finishing a stage and waiting on the next contract takes a name off the list. Stage A on that date is not a published verdict that Google cannot advance.

What the November comments add

The 6 November posts do not describe a new award. They point at a Barron's interview and repeat the Stage C claim. This page has not read the Barron's article. The X posts are the source for the ecosystem wording, the Five Eyes and NATO phrasing, and IonQ's line that it supplies systems, chips and atomic clocks to roughly a dozen other quantum firms. That supplier line is IonQ's, from its own post. A second X post the same day again cites Stage C. IonQ's 5 November earnings release, which we covered separately, had already told investors the company was in Stage C.

Analysis

A Stage C slot is an agreement to be tested, not a utility-scale computer already running. DARPA's program page says the effort is not built to crown a winner, and that DARPA estimates it is more likely than not that at least one company will have an industrially useful quantum computer by 2033. That estimate is DARPA's, and it is not a result from Stage C. The $300 million figure is a maximum on the program page and a potential contract value in IonQ's release. Later funding, IonQ says, still depends on appropriations. None of these documents reports a new qubit count or a fidelity.

Note: Stage descriptions are from DARPA's 7 October 2026 announcement and its program page. The contract ceiling, the 2029 timing and the Superion plan are IonQ's, and IonQ calls them forward-looking. This is not investment advice.
