Google's quantum algorithms chief just won one of the field's top prizes, for making chemistry problems cheaper to run

By PhotonDispatch Staff

Published 21 November 2026 · Last updated 21 November 2026

One of quantum computing's named prizes is going to Google. "Congratulations to our very own Ryan Babbush on receiving the 2027 Rolf Landauer and Charles H. Bennett Award in Quantum Computing for his vital work in quantum algorithms and circuit compilation!" Google Quantum AI posted on X on Friday.

The American Physical Society, which gives the award, announced its spring 2027 prize and award recipients earlier in the week, saying the winners "were chosen by a panel of their peers for their outstanding contributions in research, education, and public service."

## Who Babbush is

Babbush leads quantum algorithms research at Google Quantum AI. The APS award page lists him as the 2027 recipient, citing quantum algorithms and circuit compilation that enable efficient simulation of chemistry and physics.

## Why this work matters

Simulating molecules and materials is one of the main jobs people expect quantum computers to do better than classical ones. The catch is cost. A useful chemistry simulation needs a huge number of error-corrected operations, and much of the field's progress has come from finding cheaper ways to write those programs: fewer gates, fewer qubits and smarter compilation onto real hardware.

That is the kind of work the award recognises. Better algorithms move the finish line closer without any change to the hardware, which is why resource estimates for problems such as chemistry have fallen sharply over the past decade.

## About the prize

The Landauer-Bennett award is named after two IBM physicists who shaped the physics of information: Rolf Landauer, known for showing that erasing information has an energy cost, and Charles H. Bennett, a co-inventor of quantum cryptography and quantum teleportation. It honours contributions to quantum information science and computing.
