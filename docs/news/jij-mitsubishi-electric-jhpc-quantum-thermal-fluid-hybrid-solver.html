Mitsubishi Electric and Jij will test a hybrid quantum solver for thermal-fluid simulation on Japan's quantum-HPC platform

By PhotonDispatch Staff

Published 16 November 2026 · Last updated 16 November 2026

Short answer: Tokyo quantum software company Jij says a joint proposal with Mitsubishi Electric has been selected for the JHPC-quantum Test User Program, according to a press release published by The Quantum Insider on 16 November. The pair will evaluate quantum-classical hybrid algorithms for solving the linear systems that come up in thermal-fluid analysis.

The project is titled "Performance Evaluation of a Quantum-Classical Hybrid Linear Solver for Thermal-Fluid Analysis". Mitsubishi Electric is the lead proposer and Jij the joint proposer. The release says thermal-fluid simulation is used to design products ranging from transport equipment to electronic components and air-conditioning systems, and that larger, more accurate simulations are making computing efficiency a growing problem.

JHPC-quantum is a research and development project run jointly by RIKEN and SoftBank to build system software that connects quantum computers with high-performance computing systems. The test user programme invites proposals that use the platform while it is being developed, to gather feedback and identify promising applications. The work is commissioned by NEDO with support from Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry.

Tsuyoshi Yoshida, who leads Mitsubishi Electric's quantum project group, said the company wants to raise the technology's readiness level using the computing resources available. Jij chief executive Yu Yamashiro said the aim is to understand where quantum computing can make a meaningful difference on real industrial problems.

The release does not give a timeline, the size of the problems to be tested or which quantum hardware will be used. Linear solvers are a common target for hybrid methods because they sit at the heart of many engineering simulations, but useful speed-ups on today's machines are still unproven.

Note: The project is an evaluation of performance and technical challenges, not a demonstration of quantum advantage.
