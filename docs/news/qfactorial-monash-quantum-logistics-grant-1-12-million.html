QFactorial and Monash win about A$1.13 million to trial a quantum route-planning solver with freight firms

By PhotonDispatch Staff · Published 15 November 2026 · Last updated 15 November 2026

Short answer: Perth-based quantum software company QFactorial will lead a government-funded project to test a hybrid quantum-classical optimisation solver on real freight problems, with Monash University as a research partner. The official recipient list for Round 2, Stage 2 of Australia's Critical Technologies Challenge Program shows A$1,126,846 for the project, titled "Supply chain optimisation with a quantum solver for last-mile delivery".

Quantum Computing Report, which reported the award on 13 November, says it was one of six Stage 2 demonstrator projects chosen from 14 initial feasibility awards, and that the 15-month project aims to move QFactorial's routing and fleet-utilisation platform from technology readiness level 5 to level 7.

The government list names the partners as Amazon Web Services, Fujitsu Australia, Griffith University, IBM Australia, Ipec, Monash University, Opal Packaging Australia, Team Global Express and the University of Queensland. According to Quantum Computing Report, IBM Quantum, AWS and Fujitsu provide quantum and classical computing for hardware trials, while Team Global Express and Opal Packaging supply operational freight and packaging data.

Monash researchers led by Professor Muhammad Usman will work on making the algorithms more efficient and on error correction tailored to the specific circuits the solver uses, so that it holds up better against noise on current quantum processors, the outlet reports.

Vehicle routing and load planning are classic combinatorial problems where exact classical solvers can run out of time as constraints grow. The project's job is to show whether a hybrid approach can produce useful plans on real operator data within those limits; the public material does not yet include performance results.

Note: Grant figures are from the Australian government's published recipient list; the project is a demonstrator, not a commercial product.
