Alice & Bob doubles its 2030 goal to 200 logical qubits, spread across nine dilution fridges

By PhotonDispatch Staff · Published 19 November 2026 · Last updated 19 November 2026

Short answer: Alice & Bob, the Paris- and Boston-based company building superconducting cat qubits, has published a faster fault-tolerant roadmap. It now aims for 200 logical qubits from 3,600 physical qubits in a system called Carbon by 2030, double its earlier 2030 target, and 2,048 logical qubits by 2034, Quantum Computing Report says.

The ratio is the eye-catching number: 18 physical qubits per logical qubit. Cat qubits suppress bit-flip errors in the hardware itself, so the error-correction code mainly has to deal with phase flips. That is why Alice & Bob can plan for far fewer physical qubits per logical qubit than most superconducting roadmaps.

According to the technical specifications released with the roadmap, the 2030 Carbon machine would take up 350 square metres and connect nine dilution refrigerators with cryogenic coaxial cables that exchange Bell pairs between chips. It is designed around degree-five qubit connectivity, a hybrid low-density parity-check (LDPC) and repetition code, a 10 microsecond clock, 10 million logical operations and a target logical error rate of one in 10 million.

Alice & Bob says the schedule builds on work under France's PROQCIMA programme, where it built, assembled and ran three separate quantum processors and showed the core logical-qubit building blocks close to the error-correction threshold. It also credits AI-assisted chip design and algorithm work that it says cut its estimate of the resources needed to break RSA-2048 by a factor of 40. We covered its earlier RSA-2048 estimate and its GPU decoding work with NVIDIA.

The modular, multi-fridge design is the real bet. No single cryostat has to hold the whole machine, but the plan depends on the cable links between fridges working at the error rates the roadmap assumes, something no one has yet shown at this scale.

Note: This is a company roadmap. The 2030 and 2034 figures are targets, not demonstrated results.
