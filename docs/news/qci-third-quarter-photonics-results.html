Quantum Computing Inc posts $9.5 million quarterly revenue as acquisitions kick in

By PhotonDispatch Staff · Published 10 November 2026 · Last updated 10 November 2026

Quantum Computing Inc, the Hoboken, New Jersey company known as QCi, reported third quarter revenue of $9.5 million on Monday, more than the $9.2 million it booked in the whole first half of 2026. The results release shows that most of the growth came from businesses it bought this year rather than from quantum computers.

Where the revenue came from

QCi said growth was driven mainly by three companies acquired in the first half: Luminar Semiconductors, NuCrypt and NHanced Semiconductors. They sell photonic components, quantum communications products and advanced packaging services to aerospace, government and industrial customers. Revenue a year earlier was just $384 thousand.

Margins and losses

Gross profit was about $428 thousand, a gross margin of 4.5%. Operating expenses rose to $29.0 million from $10.5 million, which the company put down to higher personnel costs, expanded research and development, sales and marketing, and about $1.0 million of acquisition costs.

Interest and other income of $13.0 million helped offset that. The net loss was $15.1 million, or $0.07 per basic share.

A large cash cushion

QCi ended September with about $1.3 billion in cash, cash equivalents and investments, including $174.3 million in cash and $1.1 billion in short and long term investments. Contract backlog was about $39.1 million.

Quantum work

On the quantum side, the company highlighted a framework agreement with Hamad Bin Khalifa University in Qatar that includes cloud access to its Dirac 3 optimisation system, and a commercial agreement with INmune Bio to apply its Dirac technology alongside AI to clinical data. QCi called the INmune Bio deal its first commercial customer in pharmaceuticals and biotechnology.

Reading the numbers

The jump in revenue is real, but it reflects established photonics and packaging businesses rather than sales of quantum processors, and the 4.5% gross margin shows the combined company is still far from profitable operations. Chief executive Yuping Huang said the focus is "increasingly shifting from what we can build to what we can put to work for customers."
