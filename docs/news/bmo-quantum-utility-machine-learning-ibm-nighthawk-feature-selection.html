A Canadian bank says it hit “quantum utility” on IBM hardware, and it needed 10 to 100 times less data

By PhotonDispatch Staff · Published 20 November 2026 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Banks have talked about quantum computing for years. BMO now says it has something to show. "AI is old news: Canada's banks are now locked in a quantum computing race," The Peak posted on X on Friday, saying BMO had become "the first Canadian bank to achieve 'quantum utility'".

## What BMO announced

In its press release, BMO says it "has demonstrated quantum utility in a machine learning application using IBM quantum computers, meaning the work was performed reliably at a scale beyond what exact traditional computing methods can practically reproduce." The work was done with the Democritus University of Thrace in Greece, ran on IBM machines including the IBM Quantum Nighthawk r2 processor, and was reviewed by IBM's quantum research team, the bank says.

BMO's release does not itself use the "first Canadian bank" claim. It says the result places BMO "among a small group of global financial institutions exploring real-world quantum applications in the near term."

## How it works

The task is feature selection: working out which inputs in a large dataset actually matter for a model's prediction. BMO and DUTH adapted a framework called Proxy Quantum Games, treating each feature as a "player" that scores high when it moves with the target and low when it is uninformative or repeats other features. BMO says the method "produces results with similar quality, while only requiring one to two orders of magnitude fewer samples than traditional feature selection techniques."

## Read the fine print

"While we remain in the early stages of application development, these projects show how quantum computing can help solve business problems in new ways," said Kristin Milchanowski, BMO's chief AI and quantum officer. The bank lists client modelling and risk detection as possible future uses, not current ones.

"Quantum utility" is IBM's term for reliable results at a scale exact classical simulation can't easily reproduce. It is not the same as beating the best classical method, and BMO's release does not claim that. IBM's wider push to put its machines to work is covered in our story on [IBM, NVIDIA and Fermilab's co-design platform](/news/ibm-nvidia-fermilab-modular-quantum-co-design-platform-sc26.html).

Note: Performance claims are BMO's and were reviewed by IBM's quantum team according to BMO; no peer-reviewed paper was linked in the announcement.
